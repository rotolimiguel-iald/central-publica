[025] \subsection*{3. The frontier of three literals}
[026] Reading of the theory [INPUT/ONTO]: absolute zero pushes the solution to the projection of the opposite side; either it says yes, or it is death at absolute zero; the system inclines. The name of the stone, Trinity, refers to the frontier of three literals [INPUT]. [027] What the kernel proves, on the theory's machine:
[028] \begin{itemize}
[029] \item \textbf{The inclination does not err} (\texttt{a\_\allowbreak{}inclinacao\_\allowbreak{}nao\_\allowbreak{}erra}): propagate; die or say yes; otherwise push to the opposite side or commit to the side that cuts no clause; otherwise, the frontier.
[030] \item \textbf{It decides the Horn formulas and the two-literal formulas} (\texttt{em\_\allowbreak{}horn\_\allowbreak{}a\_\allowbreak{}inclinacao\_\allowbreak{}decide}, \texttt{em\_\allowbreak{}dois\_\allowbreak{}literais\_\allowbreak{}a\_\allowbreak{}inclinacao\_\allowbreak{}decide}), in at most (T + 1)$^3$ fixings (\texttt{o\_\allowbreak{}custo\_\allowbreak{}da\_\allowbreak{}inclinacao}). [031] \item \textbf{It stops only at the frontier of three literals} (\texttt{a\_\allowbreak{}inclinacao\_\allowbreak{}para\_\allowbreak{}so\_\allowbreak{}na\_\allowbreak{}fronteira}): when it stays undecided, it stopped at a remainder with the same answer, and at the frontier every live side cuts a clause of three literals or more.
