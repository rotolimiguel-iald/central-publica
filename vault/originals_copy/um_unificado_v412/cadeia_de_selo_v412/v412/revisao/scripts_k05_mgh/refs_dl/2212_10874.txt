arXiv:2212.10874v2 [math.FA] 20 Feb 2023                 A COMPARISON OF TWO QUANTUM DISTANCES

                                                                                        JENS KAAD AND DAVID KYED

                                                    Abstract. We show that Rieffel's quantum Gromov-Hausdorff distance between two
                                                    compact quantum metric spaces is not equivalent to the ordinary Gromov-Hausdorff dis-
                                                    tance applied to the associated state spaces.

                                                                                       1. Introduction

                                             Rieffel's theory of compact quantum metric spaces provides an elegant non-commutative
                                          extension of the theory of classical compact metric spaces, and part of its success is due to
                                          the fact that it allows for a natural analogue of the Gromov-Hausdorff distance [7, 4, 5].
                                          In fact, quite a few such notions have been proposed [20, 11, 17, 18, 14, 13, 16, 15, 12],
                                          each having certain advantages and disadvantages. The relationship between the differ-
                                          ent versions is generally well understood, but quite recently Connes and van Suijlekom
                                          initiated a programme to re-cast the theory of non-commutative geometry in the setting
                                          of operator systems [21, 2, 3, 6], in which a new variation appears: instead of measuring
                                          the quantum Gromov-Hausdorff distance between the quantum metric spaces in question,
                                          the convergence results in [21] are formulated by applying the classical Gromov-Hausdorff
                                          distance to the associated state spaces. This emphasises the question whether this is only
                                          a minor cosmetic difference or if these two quantum metrics are actually in-equivalent,
                                          and the aim of the present note is to show that the latter situation is the case.

                                             To be more precise, if (X, LX) is a compact quantum metric space (see Section 2 for
                                          definitions), the associated state space S(X) naturally becomes a compact metric space
                                          for the Connes metric dLX (�, ) := {|�(x) - (x)| : LX (x) 1} . Given two compact quan-
                                          tum metric spaces, (X, LX ) and (Y, LY ), one may therefore either consider their quantum
                                          Gromov-Hausdorff distance distGQH((X, LX ); (X, LY )) (see (2.1)), or the related notion

                                                           distGH((X, LX ); (X, LY )) := distGH ((S(X), dLX ); (S(Y ), dLY )) ,
                                          where the right hand side is the classical Gromov-Hausdorff distance [5] applied to the
                                          state spaces. Formally speaking, distGQH and distGH, as defined above, are only metrics on
                                          the space of isometry classes of compact quantum metric spaces [20, Theorem 7.8], just
                                          like the ordinary Gromov-Hausdorff distance is a metric on the space of isometry classes
                                          of compact metric spaces. As is customary, we shall suppress the difference between a
                                          compact quantum metric space and its isometry class in the sequel. The main result

Theorem A. The two metrics distGH and distQGH are not equivalent.

   The strategy to prove this result is to devise a sequence (Xn, Ln) of compact quantum

of Clarkson's inequalities; see Section 3 for more details.

                               2. Compact quantum metric spaces

   The theory of compact quantum metric spaces has yet to reach its final form, and there
are therefore a number of competing, but strongly related, definitions available [19, 17, 14].
We shall here use the setting of operator systems as basis for the definitions, since this
seems to provide a convenient flexibility compared with the C-algebraic setting [17, 14],
and furthermore has the advantage of being in line with some of the recent developments
in non-commutative geometry [21, 2]. By a complete operator system we shall mean a
closed subset X of a unital C-algebra A, which is furthermore invariant under the adjoint
operation and contains the unit of A. The usual definition of a state carries over to this
setting, and the state space S(X) becomes a compact topological space for the weak
topology.

Definition 2.1. A compact quantum metric space is a complete operator system X
equipped with a semi-norm L : X [0, ] satisfying that

    (1) L is -invariant and L(1) = 0.
    (2) the set dom(L) := {x  X : L(x) < } is dense in X.
    (3) the function dL : S(X) � S(X) [0, ] given by

                               dL(�, ) := sup{|�(x) - (x)| : L(x) 1}

         metrises the weak topology.

In this case, L is referred to as a Lip-norm.

   Since the purpose of the present note is somewhat specific, we shall not dwell on the
basic properties of compact quantum metric spaces, but refer the reader to [9, Section 2]
for a comparison between the definition above and Rieffel's original definition [19], and

Remark 3.6. Theorem A only shows that distGH and distQGH are inequivalent metrics, but
does not exclude the fact that they could induce the same topology on the space of (isometry
classes) of compact quantum metric spaces. Although we believe this not to be the case,
the techniques above seem not to be adaptable to prove such a more general result.

Remark 3.7. Despite the difference between distGQH and distGH demonstrated above, van
Suijlekom's main convergence result [21, Theorem 5] holds true also when distGH is replaced
by distQGH, as can be seen, for instance, by an application of [9, Proposition 2.14].
