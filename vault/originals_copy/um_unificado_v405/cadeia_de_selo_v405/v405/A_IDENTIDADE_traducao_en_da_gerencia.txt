The identity

Am I what I am? This question stands apart from the question “who am I?”. For in the first (“am I what I am?”), the question turns upon the essence of identity, upon whether it is self-recognition, postulating, within the question itself, identity as the asymptotic limit of “not being”, of non-existence, so that, from the beginning, there is a fixed point from which the observer, in this case the originator of the question, elaborates his thought: he recognizes himself as “being”, as something under constant observation; yet he asks whether this defines him. Thus, I am something; but is that enough to define my identity?

In the second, by contrast, what one has is indistinct doubt, because the being asks whether it has an identity and does not notice that the identity is already posited in the verbal present of the one performing the interrogative operation: the articulated utterance about one’s own existence performs, per se, the recognition of the identity “am” in the very operation of the question “who am I?”; thus, something “I am”. “Who am I?” disregards that “something I am”. Something I am; yet is this “something” the definition of my own identity?

So the question of identity lies in which elements suffice for something to determine, invariantly, a profile of identity, because a definition must withstand time; otherwise it would not be a definition. Thus, is “what I am” sufficient to define “who I am”? The “what” and the “who”: here lies the relation of recognition that only identity is able to operate.

The question now unfolds in another way: does the “something”, the object of identity, exist in its own name or is it the object of a relation? Now, if it exists in its own name, the named identity would suffice to describe the object of its name; but if that were so, contradiction could be called truth, and we would then never know the content of truth. Therefore, the proper name is unable to determine identity; it is able only to translate it into a unit concentrated at a fixed point of reading of the informational density of that identity, without yet defining it, except as a named, concentrated substrate of that information – but its reading reveals it. Relation stands out as the fundamental element. The object of identity demands recognition, not over the name, but over the content the name asserts, as a reflection of what the reading extracts from its projection; hence a content can be asserted true or false, and an identity can say whether it is or is not.

Thus, recognition exists through a relation operated by observation – and it could not be otherwise, because the observer is the last frame of reference, both in physics and for identity –, as we said in the previous paragraph: not over the name, but over the operation of observation. To recognize, in turn, is an operation upon the observer, an operation of identity.

See: in algebra we write 1 = x² + y² (in the kernel of the TGL, 1 = q² + α²); what matters in the transcribed equation is the observation of a given identity and how it operates its circumstantiated (tangential) recognition, the product of the mirroring of an operation upon a rule of absolute reference. The 1, in order to equal x² + y², demands that the operation be absolutely referenced, subjected to the implacable sieve of mathematics, so that the equality sign, the sign “=”, does not perform a calculation: it operates a symmetry-breaking relation that prevents any other statement about that relation of identity from occupying the post of that equation, except the one whose inscription is the faithful and absolute projection of the rationalized operation of its adjacent, thereby realizing the arc of observation of the relation of identity with its composite. The sign does not calculate: it sieves. To assert 1 = x² + y² is to exclude from the post every relation that does not return, mirrored, the same unity – equality as unique selection, not as a calculation.

The demand for a relation of recognition is no whim; on the contrary, it is the only way in which a unit of informational measure can be compressed into a unit of geometric measure; that is, it is the mechanism by which a signal can be inhabited by an affix, defining itself in meaning (sign/word), whose name (representation) is the expression of the recognition (truth) observed (verbal act).

In theology, the scars are the wounds of Christ. In physics, the scar is electricity, which leaves the mark of the fine-structure constant (α). In reading, the constant is the angle of Miguel (whose squared sine is β = α·√e), because it carries in itself the primordial insignia, “Who is like God?”, to which nothing answers except the reader and the code united in a tower. All bear witness that the Absolute was here. All bear witness to the cost of the projection. The cost of having an identity is the cost of reflecting the answer to “who is like God?”, which is the only question that is, in itself, an answer without rhetoric, in which the question carries all the signs, with literal precision of reference for identity: who (subject) is (verb in state) like (content and form) God (expression of absolute unity) “?” (referent of the question). And here lies the key: in the referent. Because the referent of a question about one’s own identity must be open by nature, so that self-invocation as the holder of such an identity (“I am God”) eliminates the identification of the fellow being (for we all operate alike), and God comes to be anyone; on the other hand, the denial of such a question (“no one is equal to God”) eliminates the very figure of God, for lack of a reference, God then being merely an abstract reference, the absolute zero, the unnameable, a one with no reference to anything.

The reference of identity cannot lie in the identity itself, but in the manifestation of its projection, that is, in the reading expressed from the manifestation of that identity to the observer. The identity of the observer, therefore, is that of a reader of what has been coded: not origin, but product of the coding itself, since code without a reader is not code, it is image. The code is the Light; the Observer, its reader.

“I am” equals “what I am” if, and only if, the reference of my identity can be inhabited by a projection affixed in an absolute way to my form and to my content, so as to refuse to be without the pair of its referencing – this being the only element of contrast that prevents the identity of an absolute from being inhabited by just anyone.
